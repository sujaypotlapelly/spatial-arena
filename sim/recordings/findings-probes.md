# Probe findings: what Ripple actually does

Controlled probes on the office building, run 2026-11-13 against real Ripple-65B through OpenRouter. Each probe holds a scenario fixed and changes one thing, then looks at Ripple's decisions, not just the outcomes.

- **Behavior probe** (`sim/scenarios/probe-behavior.json`): 12 setups × 4 hidden behaviors = 48 scenarios. Within a setup, the start, goal, bird start and bird settings are identical; only the behavior changes. Setups were chosen so that no single route reaches the goal in ≥3/4 seeds against all four behaviors: the right plan depends on the bird.
- **Position probe** (`sim/scenarios/probe-position.json`): 10 setups × 3–4 ambush points = 37 scenarios. Within a setup, only the bird's waiting point changes, and each point sits on a different route.
- Harnesses: **A** (raw) and **H** (structured steps + learned strategy from `ripple-training.json`). One run each, temperature 0.3, 4 seeds per decision.
- Generated by `sim/tools/legacy/make_probes.mjs`; flown by `sim/tools/legacy/harness_eval.mjs`; summarized by `sim/tools/legacy/probe_report.mjs`. Raw sessions: `runs/probes/behavior.json`, `runs/probes/position.json` (gitignored).
- Invalid decisions: 0/96 (behavior), 2/74 (position). The upstream API failed ~40% of call attempts during the run; calls were retried up to 9 times, so failures did not become invalid decisions.

## 1. Ripple's reasoning chain is coherent; the weak link is reading motion

With structured steps (H), Ripple first writes the bird's velocity and whether it is moving toward the drone, then guesses its behavior. Its guess follows its own geometric answer almost exactly:

| Ripple's own answer | Its guess |
|---|---|
| "bird is moving toward me" (25) | pursue 22, patrol 2, intercept 1 |
| "bird is not moving toward me" (23) | patrol 16, ambush 5, intercept 2 |

The errors come from the geometry step, not the reasoning after it:

| | Moving birds (behavior probe) | Stationary birds (position probe) |
|---|---|---|
| "Moving toward me?" correct | 33/48 (69%) | 36/36 (100%) |
| Velocity estimate error | 0.98 m/s (true speed ~2.8 m/s) | 0.04 m/s |

Every pursuing or intercepting bird was closing at ~3 m/s during the observation, yet Ripple said "not moving toward me" for 9 of those 24.

**Claim:** Ripple reasons in the right order and acts on its conclusions. What limits it is turning a short list of coordinates into a motion estimate. This is consistent with the 36-scenario result that geometry tools cut 3 s prediction error from 7.6 m to 5.4 m.

## 2. It reads a waiting bird, not a bird's intent

| | Bird already sitting at its post | Bird flying to its post |
|---|---|---|
| Identified as ambush | A 35/36, H 34/36 (position probe) | 5/24 pooled (behavior probe) |

**Claim:** Ripple's ambush detection is "the bird is not moving", not "the bird is heading somewhere to wait". When the ambusher is still travelling, Ripple calls it pursue or patrol.

## 3. It routes away from where the bird is

Position probe, 70 valid decisions:

| | Picked the route the bird waits on | Route's distance from the bird |
|---|---|---|
| Ripple A | 0/35 | 11.8 m |
| Ripple H | 0/35 | 12.2 m |
| Random route | 13% expected | 9.4 m |
| Bird-aware heuristic (max clearance) | n/a | 12.5 m |
| Farthest route available | n/a | 13.6 m |

**Claim:** Ripple never sends the drone down the route the bird is guarding, and picks routes nearly as far from the bird as a planner built to maximize that distance.

Caveat: only 1 of 37 position scenarios put the bird on the shortest route, so shortest path also scores 76% here. This probe shows route avoidance, not a success advantage.

## 4. Its plan doesn't follow its belief

Behavior probe:

- **Settings barely change with the guess.** Speed 4.6–5.0 m/s and evade distance 7.0–7.4 m whether it guesses pursue, intercept, ambush or patrol.
- **Route changes aren't tied to the guess.** Within a setup, Ripple's route differs between two behaviors 52% of the time when its guess differs and 54% when its guess is the same (A); 58% vs. 53% for H.
- **Route quality is near chance.** It picks a route that works against the bird's true behavior (≥3/4 with a reference controller) 50% of the time. Random route: 47%. Shortest route: 52%. Bird-aware heuristic: 58%.

**Claim:** Ripple states a belief about the bird, but its route and settings don't depend on it. That is why better bird-reading has not turned into more successful flights anywhere in this project.

Caveat: one run at temperature 0.3, so some route variation is sampling noise; a repeat run would separate the two.

## 5. Outcomes when the bird's behavior decides the right plan

| Behavior probe | Success |
|---|---|
| Shortest path (ignores the bird) | 36% |
| Ripple A (raw) | 57% |
| Ripple H (structured + learned strategy) | 55% |
| Bird-aware heuristic | 63% |

The heuristic drops from 79% on the regular 12-scenario test set to 63% here; Ripple stays above shortest path and below the heuristic. The learned strategy doesn't help on this probe, and H's family accuracy (35%) is lower than A's (50%, which is chance for two families).

## 6. A limit in our own benchmark

While the drone hovers during the 2 s observation, an intercepting bird aims at the drone's current position, so its track is identical to a pursuing bird's. Measured on every scenario:

| | Max difference in observed track |
|---|---|
| Pursue vs. intercept, probe set | 0.00 m |
| Pursue vs. intercept, 36-scenario held-out set | 0.00 m |
| Ambush vs. patrol, 36-scenario held-out set | mean 1.07 m, max 8.9 m |

**Consequence:** no model can tell pursue from intercept in this setup, so "exact behavior identified" has a ceiling well below 100%, and Ripple rarely guessing "intercept" is the right call, not a blind spot. The behavior-family metric (chase vs. wait) is the fair one. To make intercept identifiable, the drone would need to move during the observation.
